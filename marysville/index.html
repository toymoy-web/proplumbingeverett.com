Geography

Marysville Sits Across the Snohomish River Delta From Everett

Marysville is the second-largest city in Snohomish County, with 70,714 residents at the 2020 census, and it sits directly north of Everett on the north side of the Snohomish River delta. Ebey Slough runs along the city's southern side, Quilceda Creek winds through it, and the Tulalip Reservation borders it to the west. Interstate 5 runs north and south through the city, with State Route 9 along its eastern side.

From our Everett base it is roughly 6 to 8 miles by road, usually 10 to 15 minutes depending on I-5 traffic. That makes Marysville one of the closest communities we serve and a regular stop on our daily dispatch route rather than an add-on trip.

Housing

The Historic Core and the Post-1980 Growth Around It

Marysville was incorporated in 1891 as a timber town on the slough, and strawberry farming later earned it the nickname Strawberry City, with the annual Strawberry Festival running since 1932. The older blocks around State Avenue and Comeford Park come from that era, which is why we plan for original galvanized supply lines and clay sewer laterals when we are called to an older home in the core.

Most of the city looks very different. Marysville's population grew about fivefold between 1980 and 2000, and annexations in the early 2000s pushed it past 60,000, so the majority of its housing is newer subdivisions around the old center. Newer homes usually mean newer supply piping, which shifts the typical call toward water heaters, garbage disposals, fixtures, and drain cleaning rather than old-pipe replacement.

Local Signature

Delta Ground, Sloughs, and the Calls Wet Weather Brings

Low ground along Ebey Slough, Quilceda Creek, and the delta stays wet for much of the rainy season, and that shows up in the plumbing calls. Sump pump failures, crawlspace moisture, and drains that back up when the soil is saturated are the pattern we plan for on low-lying lots, while lots on higher ground see far fewer of them.

If a line has backed up more than once, we run a camera before recommending a repair, because on older lateral pipe the cause is often roots or a failed joint rather than a simple clog. Any replacement section is installed to the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Washington State. See our drain cleaning and sewer line repair pages for how that works.

Coverage

ZIP 98270 and 98271: What Each Part of Marysville Calls For

ZIP 98270 covers central Marysville, including the downtown State Avenue area, and holds the larger share of the city's residents. Homes there range from the historic core to subdivisions built over the last four decades, so calls split between older-pipe repairs near the center and water heater, drain, and fixture work across the rest.

ZIP 98271 covers the northern and western side of the city and also carries the Quil Ceda Village and Tulalip mailing names. We expect newer-system service there, mainly water heater repair and drain cleaning, and we confirm access and any local permitting up front for addresses on Tulalip Tribes land.

Our dispatch also reaches the surrounding ZIP codes, including 98201 and 98203 in Everett to the south, 98258 in Lake Stevens to the southeast, and 98223 in Arlington to the north. The work is the same water heater, drain cleaning, and sump pump work Marysville itself calls for.

Emergency Prep

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Marysville

  • Shut off your main water valve immediately if a pipe bursts, then call.
  • If your home sits near Ebey Slough or Quilceda Creek, check the crawlspace and any sump basin for standing water after heavy rain.
  • Older homes near State Avenue and Comeford Park often still have galvanized supply lines, so expect more than one weak spot if one pipe fails.
  • Photograph any water damage for insurance purposes once the water is off.
  • Avoid running water in any fixture that has already started to back up.
  • Have your ZIP code (98270 or 98271) ready when you call so we can confirm the dispatch route.
