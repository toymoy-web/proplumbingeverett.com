Sump Pump Installation & Repair in Everett, WA

A sump pump is the one appliance in an Everett basement that only has to work during the worst storm of the year, which is exactly when a marginal unit fails.

Call (425) 845-0257 See FAQs

Everett's rainy season puts more demand on a sump pump than almost anywhere else in Western Washington, and a pump that's undersized, aging, or wired to a single point of failure will show it at the worst possible time, during the heaviest storm of the winter. Sump pump installation and repair is one of the most requested services alongside basement waterproofing in this city, and the two problems are closely related.

We repair and replace both primary sump pumps and battery backup systems, and we size every replacement to the property's actual water volume rather than swapping in an identical unit that already proved it can't keep up.

Local Cause

Why Sump Pumps Fail During Everett's Wet Season

Most sump pump failures we see in Everett fall into three categories: a float switch that sticks or wears out, a pump motor that burns out after running near-continuously during a multi-day storm, and power loss during the exact storms that cause the flooding in the first place. Older homes in neighborhoods with a high water table, like areas near the Snohomish River valley or low-lying sections of the city, put the heaviest seasonal load on these systems.

A pump that was correctly sized for a normal winter can still be overwhelmed during an unusually wet stretch, which is one reason a battery backup system has become a standard recommendation rather than an optional upgrade for basements with a real flooding history.

Most residential sump pumps are rated in gallons per minute at a specific lift height, and a pump sized for a shallow basin in a typical suburban lot may not keep pace with a deeper basin or a higher water table closer to the Snohomish River valley. Matching pump capacity to the property's actual conditions, not just replacing like-for-like, is what prevents the same failure from repeating.

Signs

Signs Your Sump Pump Is About to Fail

  • The pump runs constantly instead of cycling on and off with rising water.
  • A grinding, rattling, or unusually loud motor sound when the pump activates.
  • The pump doesn't activate at all when water reaches the float switch level.
  • Visible rust or corrosion on the pump housing or discharge pipe fittings.
  • The unit is more than 7-10 years old and has never been tested or serviced.
Method

Testing a Sump Pump Before It's an Emergency

We test the float switch mechanically, confirm the check valve on the discharge line isn't stuck or backflowing, and calculate the pump's actual gallons-per-minute capacity against the basin's real water volume during a heavy rain event. A pump that's technically working but undersized for the property will still flood a basement, just more slowly than a fully failed one.

For homes with a documented flooding history, we also check whether the discharge line routes water far enough from the foundation, since a pump that's simply recirculating water back toward the house is fighting a losing battle regardless of its condition.

Options

Repair, Battery Backup, or Full Replacement

A stuck float switch or a failed check valve is often a straightforward repair on an otherwise sound pump. A motor that's burned out or a unit that's undersized for the property's real water volume usually calls for full replacement rather than a patch repair.

For basements with a real flooding history, we recommend a battery backup system that activates automatically during a power outage, since Everett's worst storms are also the ones most likely to knock out power. This pairs directly with any broader basement waterproofing work already in place.

Pricing

What Affects Sump Pump Cost in Everett

Cost Factors

  • Pump capacity: a higher-GPM pump for a high-water-table property costs more than a standard unit.
  • Battery backup: adding backup power roughly doubles the system cost but prevents power-outage flooding.
  • Existing basin condition: a damaged or undersized basin may need replacement alongside the pump.
  • Discharge line routing: rerouting a line that dumps water back near the foundation adds labor.
  • Access: a finished basement adds labor time compared to an open, unfinished space.

Sump pump failure and resulting water damage is often excluded or capped under standard homeowner's policies unless a specific sump pump overflow endorsement was added. Check your policy before assuming coverage.

Before You Call

What to Check Before the Next Storm

  • Pour a bucket of water into the sump basin now to confirm the pump activates and drains properly.
  • Check that the discharge line outside the house is clear and not frozen, blocked, or disconnected.
  • Listen for grinding or unusual noise during the test, which signals a failing motor before total failure.
  • Confirm the pump is plugged into a dedicated outlet, not a shared circuit that could trip during a storm.
  • If you don't have a battery backup and your basement has flooded before, plan that upgrade before winter.
Questions

Sump Pump Questions

How often should a sump pump be replaced?

Most sump pumps last 7-10 years under normal use. Everett's heavy seasonal demand can shorten that, so we recommend testing annually before the rainy season regardless of the unit's age.

Do I need a battery backup system?

If your basement has flooded during a power outage before, or if flooding would cause serious damage, yes. Everett's worst storms frequently combine heavy rain with power loss.

Is a sump pump repair the same as basement waterproofing?

No. A sump pump is one component of a waterproofing system. If drainage around the foundation is also failing, see our basement waterproofing page for the full picture.

Do you offer 24/7 emergency sump pump service?

Yes. A failed pump during an active storm is treated as an emergency call, day or night.

Can I install a battery backup on my existing pump?

In many cases, yes. A battery backup system can often be added to a sound existing pump rather than requiring full replacement, which keeps the upgrade cost lower for a pump that's otherwise working well.

How long does a battery backup last during an outage?

This depends on the battery size and how often the pump cycles during the outage, but most residential systems are sized to handle a multi-hour outage during an active storm, which covers the majority of real power losses in this area.

Do you serve areas outside Everett?

Yes, including Lake Stevens, Snohomish, Mukilteo, Mill Creek, and Edmonds.

What if my basement already flooded before the pump could be fixed?

See our water mitigation page for immediate water extraction and drying, a separate service from the pump repair itself.
