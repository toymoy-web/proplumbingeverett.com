Water Damage Restoration in Everett, WA

Water damage restoration is a distinct service from plumbing repair: it's the cleanup, drying, and rebuild after water has already gotten into your home, not the fix for the pipe that caused it.

Call (833) 521-1033 See FAQs

Water damage restoration addresses what happens after water has already entered a home: soaked flooring and drywall, saturated insulation, and the real risk of mold growth if drying doesn't start within the first 24-48 hours. This is a different service category from fixing the burst pipe, failed water heater, or storm intrusion that caused the water in the first place. If you also need the plumbing itself repaired, see our burst pipe repair page, since that work is billed and scheduled separately from restoration.

Everett's long rainy season and its share of pre-1970s homes with aging drainage and plumbing make water damage a real, recurring event here, not a rare emergency. We run 24/7 restoration dispatch specifically because water damage gets measurably worse with every hour drying is delayed.

Local Cause

Why Water Damage Is So Common in Everett

Two local factors drive most of the water damage restoration calls we see. First, Everett's October-through-April rainy season raises groundwater pressure against older foundations, and a failed drain tile system or undersized sump pump lets water into a basement faster than most homeowners expect. Second, homes built before 1970 commonly still run original galvanized supply lines, which corrode from the inside out and can fail suddenly after decades of gradual narrowing.

A burst pipe, a failed water heater, or a basement flood all create the same underlying problem once the water is on the floor: saturated materials that need to be dried out fast, and in some cases removed and replaced, before mold has a chance to establish.

Signs

Signs You Need Professional Restoration, Not Just Cleanup

  • Standing water that's been present for more than a few hours.
  • Saturated carpet, drywall, or insulation that feels damp days after the water event.
  • A musty smell developing in an affected area within 24-48 hours.
  • Visible warping or buckling in flooring or baseboards.
  • Water damage affecting more than a small, easily-mopped area.
Method

How We Assess Water Damage

We assess the extent of water intrusion using moisture meters to check how far water has traveled into walls, subfloor, and other building materials, not just what's visible on the surface. This matters because water wicks into drywall and framing well beyond the visibly wet area, and drying only the obvious wet spot leaves hidden moisture that causes mold weeks later.

We also identify the water category, clean water from a supply line versus contaminated water from a sewer backup, since that classification changes what materials can be dried in place versus what needs to be removed and replaced.

Options

Drying, Removal, and Rebuild

For clean-water intrusion caught early, industrial air movers and dehumidifiers can often dry structural materials in place over several days, avoiding the need to remove drywall or flooring entirely. For water that's been present longer, or contaminated water from a sewer backup, affected materials typically need to be removed and replaced rather than dried, since porous materials that have absorbed contaminated water can't be fully sanitized.

Our drying and restoration process follows IICRC S500 water damage restoration standards, the industry-recognized reference for structural drying, which specify moisture-monitoring benchmarks before a space is considered dry rather than relying on a visual guess.

Pricing

What Affects Water Damage Restoration Cost

Cost Factors

  • Water category: clean water restoration costs less than contaminated water requiring material removal.
  • Extent: a single affected room costs less than multi-room or multi-floor damage.
  • Response time: water damage caught within 24-48 hours typically costs less to restore than delayed response.
  • Materials affected: drywall, flooring, and insulation each have different removal and replacement costs.
  • Drying time: structural drying is billed by the day, so faster equipment deployment reduces total cost.

Sudden water damage from a burst pipe or storm event is commonly covered under homeowner's insurance. Gradual damage from a long-term undetected leak is typically not covered. We can provide documentation for your insurance claim regardless of coverage outcome.

Before You Call

What to Do Before We Arrive

  • Shut off the water source if the cause is an active plumbing failure, then call.
  • Turn off electricity to any affected area if water is near outlets or fixtures.
  • Photograph the damage for insurance purposes before moving anything.
  • Move valuables and furniture away from standing water if it's safe to do so.
  • Avoid using a household vacuum on standing water, which creates an electrical hazard.

Water Damage Restoration Questions

Do you offer 24/7 emergency response?

Yes. Water damage restoration is time-sensitive, so we dispatch around the clock.

Is this the same as fixing the plumbing that caused the flood?

No. Restoration addresses the water damage itself. See our burst pipe repair or basement waterproofing pages for the plumbing-side fix, which is billed separately.

Will my insurance cover this?

Sudden damage from a burst pipe or storm is commonly covered. Gradual, long-term leaks typically are not. We document the damage regardless, which supports your claim either way.

How long does drying take?

Clean-water structural drying typically takes 3-5 days with proper equipment. Contaminated water requiring material removal can take longer depending on scope.
