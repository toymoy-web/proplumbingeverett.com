Garbage Disposal Repair & Installation in Everett, WA

A garbage disposal that hums but won't spin, or one that's leaking at the sink connection, are two of the most common calls we get, and they have very different fixes.

Call (425) 845-0257 See FAQs

Garbage disposal problems generally fall into a few clear categories: a jam that stops the unit from spinning, a leak at one of the plumbing connections, or a unit that's simply reached the end of its 10-12 year service life. Diagnosing which one you're dealing with determines whether a quick fix or a full replacement makes sense.

A disposal that hums but doesn't spin usually means the flywheel is jammed, not that the motor has failed, which is an important distinction since one is a five-minute fix and the other means replacement.

Local Cause

Why Disposals Fail: Jams, Leaks, and Age

Most jams come from fibrous food waste, like celery or corn husks, or from something hard and non-food getting into the unit accidentally. Leaks typically occur at one of three points: the sink flange seal, the dishwasher connection, or the discharge pipe connecting to the drain line.

Units installed in homes with original, narrower drain lines can also experience more frequent downstream clogging, since the ground food waste has less pipe diameter to move through before reaching the main line.

Signs

Signs Your Disposal Needs Attention

  • A humming sound with no spinning when the disposal is switched on.
  • Water leaking from underneath the disposal unit at the sink.
  • Unusual grinding or rattling noise during normal operation.
  • Frequent tripping of the reset button on the unit itself.
  • Persistent bad odor even after regular cleaning.
Method

Diagnosing a Disposal Problem

For a humming, non-spinning unit, we first check for a jam at the flywheel, often clearable with the manual reset wrench that comes with most disposals, before assuming the motor has failed.

For a leak, we isolate which of the three common connection points is the source, since a leak at the sink flange requires a different repair than one at the dishwasher line or discharge pipe.

Options

Repair, Reset, or Full Replacement

A jam is typically cleared and the unit reset without needing replacement parts. A leaking seal at a specific connection point is usually a straightforward gasket or seal replacement.

For a unit with a failed motor, persistent leaking despite seal repairs, or one that's simply past its typical 10-12 year service life, full replacement is usually the better value than continuing to repair an aging unit.

A disposal leak at the flange seal is sometimes mistaken for a faucet or sink supply line leak since both show up as moisture under the sink. We check both during the same visit rather than assuming the source before confirming it.

Pricing

What Affects Garbage Disposal Cost

Cost Factors

  • Repair type: clearing a jam costs far less than replacing a leaking seal or full unit replacement.
  • Unit horsepower: replacement units vary in cost based on motor size and features.
  • Connection type: matching an existing dishwasher and drain configuration affects installation time.
  • Age: a unit near the end of its service life often isn't worth repairing repeatedly.
  • Downstream issues: a disposal leak sometimes reveals a separate drain line problem underneath.

Garbage disposal repair or replacement is a standard maintenance expense, not typically covered under homeowner's insurance.

Before You Call

What to Check Before You Call

  • Check if the unit hums without spinning, which usually means a jam, not a failed motor.
  • Look under the sink for visible leaking at the disposal's connection points.
  • Try the manual reset button on the bottom of the unit before assuming it needs replacement.
  • Note the unit's approximate age, since units over 10-12 years often warrant replacement over repair.
  • Avoid running the disposal further if it's making a grinding sound with something visibly stuck inside.
Questions

Garbage Disposal Questions

Why does my disposal hum but not spin?

This almost always means the flywheel is jammed, not that the motor has failed. It's usually clearable with the manual reset wrench in a few minutes.

How long do garbage disposals typically last?

Most units last 10-12 years with normal use. Past that point, repeated repairs usually cost more over time than replacement.

Can you match my existing dishwasher connection?

Yes, we match the existing configuration or update it as needed during installation.

Is a leaking disposal an emergency?

Not typically, though it should be addressed promptly to avoid water damage to the cabinet below.

Do you serve areas outside Everett?

Yes, including Snohomish, Mukilteo, Lake Stevens, Mill Creek, and Edmonds.
