Tankless Water Heater Service in Everett, WA

A tankless water heater needs different maintenance than a tank system, and skipping it shortens the unit's lifespan faster than most owners expect.

Call (425) 845-0257 See FAQs

Tankless water heaters trade a large storage tank for an on-demand heating element that only fires when hot water is actually needed, which saves both space and standby energy loss. What a lot of new tankless owners don't realize is that these systems need regular descaling to keep working correctly, something a traditional tank system doesn't require in the same way.

If you're hearing your tankless unit make new noises, noticing inconsistent water temperature, or you've simply never had it serviced since installation, that's usually the underlying issue.

Local Cause

Why Tankless Systems Need Descaling in Snohomish County

Even moderately hard water leaves mineral scale on a tankless unit's heat exchanger over time, since the water passes directly across a heating element rather than sitting in a tank. That scale buildup reduces heating efficiency and, left unaddressed, can shorten the unit's expected 15-20 year service life significantly.

We're seeing more tankless installations in Everett's newer construction and in homes upgrading from an aging tank system, and the most common service call we get on these units is a system that's simply never been descaled since installation, sometimes years earlier.

Signs

Signs Your Tankless System Needs Service

  • Inconsistent water temperature that fluctuates during a single shower or use.
  • Reduced hot water flow rate compared to when the system was new.
  • An error code displayed on the unit's control panel.
  • Unusual popping or rumbling sounds during operation.
  • It's been more than 12 months since the last descaling service.
Method

Diagnosing a Tankless System Problem

We check the error code history on the unit's control panel first, since most manufacturers use specific codes that point directly to a scale buildup issue, a flow sensor problem, or a venting fault. From there, a descaling flush using a circulation pump and descaling solution clears mineral buildup from the heat exchanger.

For units showing a flow-related error, we also check the inlet water filter, which can become restricted with sediment over time and mimic a scaling problem even when the heat exchanger itself is clean.

Options

Descaling, Component Repair, or Replacement

Annual descaling is the standard maintenance service for most households and resolves the majority of performance complaints. A failed flow sensor, igniter, or circuit board is a component-level repair on an otherwise sound unit.

Tankless units are more repairable over a longer lifespan than tank systems, so full replacement is less common unless the unit has a history of repeated component failures or was undersized for the household from installation.

Not Sure What's Wrong?

Think It Needs a Service, But Not Sure What's Wrong?

Callers often describe this as "it needs a flush" or "I think it's time for a cleaning" without knowing the specific cause. In most cases, that instinct is correct: scale buildup on the heat exchanger is the single most common reason a tankless system's performance degrades over time, and a descaling flush resolves it in the majority of cases without needing part replacement.

Pricing

What Affects Tankless Service Cost

Cost Factors

  • Service type: routine descaling costs less than diagnosing and repairing a specific component failure.
  • Water hardness: households on well water or with harder municipal supply need more frequent descaling.
  • Access: units installed in tight utility closets take longer to service.
  • Age and history: a unit that's never been serviced may need a longer initial descaling pass.
  • Part availability: some manufacturer-specific components may require ordering.

Routine maintenance like descaling is a homeowner expense, not an insurance matter. Sudden component failure causing water damage may fall under your homeowner's policy separately.

Before You Call

What to Check Before You Call

  • Check the unit's control panel for an error code and note it before calling.
  • Note how long it's been since the last descaling service, if you know.
  • Check whether the issue is inconsistent temperature or reduced flow, since these point to different causes.
  • If the unit is completely unresponsive, check the breaker or gas shutoff first.
  • Note whether you're on municipal water or a private well, since that affects service frequency.
Questions

Tankless Water Heater Questions

How often does a tankless system need descaling?

Annually for most households on municipal water. Homes with harder water or well water may need it more often.

Can I switch from a tank system to tankless?

Yes, in most cases. See our water heater installation & replacement page for what a conversion involves.

Is a tankless system worth the higher upfront cost?

For many households, yes, over the system's longer 15-20 year lifespan and lower standby energy loss, though the payback timeline depends on household hot water usage.

What does an error code on my unit mean?

It depends on the manufacturer and code, but many point directly to scale buildup or a flow restriction. Tell us the code when you call for a faster diagnosis.

Do you serve areas outside Everett?

Yes, including Mill Creek, Snohomish, Lake Stevens, Mukilteo, and Edmonds.
