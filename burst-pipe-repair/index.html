Burst Pipe Repair in Everett, WA

A burst pipe is a plumbing repair, not a water damage restoration job, even when it also causes water damage elsewhere in the house. We fix the pipe fast, day or night.

Call (425) 845-0257 See FAQs

A burst or actively leaking pipe is one of the few true plumbing emergencies where minutes matter. Everett's stock of pre-1970 galvanized steel supply lines is a real, documented risk factor here: galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out over decades, and by the time it fails, the interior diameter has often narrowed and weakened well before the visible burst happens.

Our emergency response starts with stopping the water, then locating and repairing the actual failure point, whether that's a single fitting, a section of corroded galvanized pipe, or a sign that the whole supply line needs broader attention.

Local Cause

Why Everett's Older Pipes Are More Likely to Burst

Galvanized steel pipe, standard practice in homes built before 1970, has a real service life of roughly 40-50 years before internal corrosion becomes a structural risk. A large share of Everett's older housing stock, particularly around downtown and the established neighborhoods near Rucker Hill, is now well past that window. A pipe that's been slowly narrowing from rust buildup for decades can fail suddenly under normal water pressure with no advance warning.

Occasional hard freezes, while less common in Everett's marine climate than in colder inland areas, can still crack an already-weakened section of pipe in an uninsulated crawlspace or exterior wall. That's why we see a real uptick in burst pipe calls during the rare cold snaps that do hit the area.

Water pressure fluctuation is a less obvious contributor. A municipal pressure spike, common during certain maintenance events or after a nearby water main repair, puts sudden stress on already-thin pipe walls that would handle normal pressure without issue. This is one reason a pipe can fail seemingly out of nowhere on an otherwise unremarkable day.

Signs

Signs of a Hidden Pipe Failure

  • A sudden, unexplained spike in your water bill.
  • A hissing or dripping sound inside a wall or under a floor.
  • A warm or damp spot on a wall, floor, or ceiling with no obvious cause.
  • Reduced water pressure at a single fixture or throughout the house.
  • Visible water pooling near a foundation wall or crawlspace access point.
Method

Locating a Burst or Failing Pipe

For a visible leak, the shutoff and repair are usually immediate. For a suspected hidden leak behind a wall or under a slab, we use acoustic leak detection equipment to pinpoint the exact location before opening any wall or flooring, rather than guessing based on where water happens to be showing up.

We also check the surrounding section of pipe for additional corrosion once we've located the primary failure, since a galvanized line that's failed in one spot is frequently close to failing elsewhere too.

Options

Spot Repair, Section Replacement, or Full Repipe

A single failure point in an otherwise sound section of pipe usually gets a direct spot repair. If the surrounding pipe shows significant corrosion, replacing that full section prevents the next failure from happening a few feet away within months.

For a home with galvanized supply lines throughout and a documented history of repeat pipe failures, a full whole-home repipe often costs less over time than repairing the same aging system fixture by fixture. We'll walk through that comparison honestly if it applies to your home.

After any repair, we pressure test the repaired section before closing up a wall or backfilling a trench. A repair that holds at standard test pressure but was never actually verified is how a second, avoidable emergency call happens a few months later, and pressure testing catches that before we leave rather than after.

Pricing

What Affects Burst Pipe Repair Cost in Everett

Cost Factors

  • Access: a pipe behind drywall or under a slab costs more to reach than an exposed crawlspace line.
  • Pipe material: galvanized repairs often reveal more corrosion once opened up than copper or PEX.
  • Timing: after-hours emergency dispatch is priced differently than a scheduled daytime repair.
  • Scope: a single spot repair costs far less than a section replacement or full repipe.
  • Water damage: standing water from the burst may require separate cleanup outside standard plumbing repair.

Sudden, accidental water damage from a burst pipe is typically covered under standard homeowner's policies. Gradual leaks from long-term corrosion are usually not covered, which is one reason catching a slow leak early matters.

Before You Call

What to Do in the First Five Minutes

  • Shut off the main water valve immediately, before calling, to limit the damage.
  • Turn off electricity to any affected area if water is near outlets or fixtures.
  • Call us right away once the water is off, since stopping the flow matters more than call speed.
  • Photograph the damage for insurance purposes once it's safe to do so.
  • Move valuables and furniture away from the affected area if it's safe to do so.
Questions

Burst Pipe Questions

How fast can you respond to a burst pipe?

We treat burst pipes as true emergencies with same-day, around-the-clock dispatch. Shut off your main water valve first, then call.

Is a burst pipe covered by insurance?

Sudden, accidental bursts are typically covered under standard homeowner's policies. Gradual corrosion-related leaks usually are not. Check your specific policy.

Do I need a full repipe or just a spot repair?

It depends on how much of the surrounding pipe shows corrosion. A single isolated failure often needs only a spot repair; a home with repeated failures may benefit from a full repipe.

What if I can't find my main shutoff valve?

Locate it now, before an emergency. It's typically near where the water line enters the house, often in a basement, crawlspace, or utility closet.

Can a burst pipe be prevented?

Not entirely, but replacing visibly corroded galvanized sections before they fail, and insulating exposed pipe in unheated crawlspaces, meaningfully reduces the risk.

Will homeowner's insurance cover the water damage too?

Often, yes, for sudden accidental damage from the burst itself, though coverage for the pipe repair versus the resulting water damage cleanup can fall under different parts of a policy. Check with your insurer directly.

Do you serve areas outside Everett?

Yes, including Edmonds, Mukilteo, Snohomish, Lake Stevens, and Mill Creek.
