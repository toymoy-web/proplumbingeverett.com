Shower & Bathtub Installation in Everett, WA

A shower or bathtub replacement is as much a plumbing project as a design one, especially in older homes where the valve and drain configuration needs updating along with the fixture.

Call (425) 845-0257 See FAQs

Shower and bathtub installation covers full fixture replacement, converting a tub to a walk-in shower, and upgrading valves and drain assemblies during a bathroom remodel. In Everett's older homes, this work often involves more than swapping the visible fixture, since original valve and drain configurations may not meet current code or may simply be worn out after decades of use.

We coordinate the plumbing side of a shower or tub installation, working alongside any tile or general contracting work happening as part of a broader bathroom remodel.

Local Cause

What Changes When Updating an Older Everett Bathroom

Homes with original 1950s-60s bathroom plumbing often have single-handle or two-handle valve assemblies that don't include modern anti-scald protection, a real safety upgrade worth making during any fixture replacement rather than reusing outdated valve hardware behind a new tub or shower surround.

Drain assemblies in older bathrooms can also be undersized or made from materials, like older cast iron, that don't match modern fixture drain requirements, which is why we assess the full plumbing connection, not just the visible fixture, before any installation.

Signs

Signs It's Time for Shower or Tub Replacement

  • A cracked, chipped, or heavily worn tub or shower surface.
  • Persistent leaking around the fixture despite caulking and sealant repairs.
  • An outdated valve without anti-scald or pressure-balancing protection.
  • A desire to convert an underused bathtub into a walk-in shower.
  • Water damage to surrounding flooring or walls from long-term fixture leaking.
Method

Assessing the Plumbing Behind the Fixture

Before installation, we check the existing valve, supply lines, and drain assembly for condition and code compliance, since replacing just the visible tub or shower without addressing outdated plumbing behind it often means redoing the work again sooner than expected.

For a tub-to-shower conversion specifically, we confirm the drain location and slope work correctly for the new fixture's footprint, since a shower pan has different drainage requirements than a standard tub.

Options

Fixture Replacement, Valve Upgrade, or Full Conversion

A straightforward like-for-like fixture replacement is the simplest option when the existing valve and drain are in good condition and meet current code. Adding a modern anti-scald valve during replacement is a worthwhile upgrade even when not strictly required.

A full tub-to-shower conversion involves more plumbing work, relocating or reconfiguring the drain and supply lines for the new fixture type, and we'll walk through what that specifically requires for your bathroom's existing layout.

Either project is a good opportunity to also address the water supply lines feeding the fixture, particularly in homes still running original galvanized branch lines, since opening the wall for fixture work is the least disruptive time to update that piping.

Pricing

What Affects Installation Cost

Cost Factors

  • Scope: a like-for-like fixture swap costs less than a full tub-to-shower conversion.
  • Valve condition: replacing an outdated valve adds cost but improves safety and reliability.
  • Drain work: relocating or reconfiguring drain plumbing for a conversion adds labor.
  • Fixture material and quality: acrylic, fiberglass, and tile-ready options vary significantly in cost.
  • Access: bathroom layout and any needed wall access affect labor time.

Fixture replacement and upgrades are homeowner improvement expenses, not covered by insurance, unless replacement is necessitated by a covered water damage event.

Before You Call

What to Decide Before Installation

  • Decide whether you want a like-for-like replacement or a tub-to-shower conversion.
  • Check whether your current valve has anti-scald or pressure-balancing protection.
  • Note any visible water damage to surrounding walls or flooring that may need separate repair.
  • Consider fixture material options based on your budget and maintenance preferences.
  • Coordinate timing if this is part of a larger bathroom remodel with other contractors.
Questions

Shower & Bathtub Installation Questions

Do I need to replace my valve if I'm just replacing the tub?

Not always, but we recommend assessing valve condition and code compliance during any fixture replacement, since it's far more disruptive to address later.

How long does a tub-to-shower conversion take?

The plumbing portion is typically completed in one to two days, though the full project timeline depends on coordination with tile and general contracting work.

Can you install a shower where a tub currently is?

Yes, this is one of the most common requests we get, and it involves reconfiguring the drain for the shower pan's requirements.

Do you work with contractors on full bathroom remodels?

Yes, we coordinate the plumbing scope alongside general contracting, tile, and other remodel work.

Do you serve areas outside Everett?

Yes, including Lake Stevens, Snohomish, Mukilteo, Mill Creek, and Edmonds.
