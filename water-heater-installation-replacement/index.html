Water Heater Installation & Replacement in Everett, WA

A full water heater replacement is a different job than a repair, especially in Everett's older homes where the original closet was built around a smaller-capacity unit.

Call (425) 845-0257 See FAQs

When a tank water heater has reached the end of its service life, replacement is usually the better financial decision over another round of repairs. In Everett, that decision comes with a wrinkle a lot of homeowners don't expect: many pre-1970 water heater closets were sized and vented for a smaller, older-generation tank, and installing a modern higher-capacity unit without addressing clearance and venting can create a real code and safety issue.

We handle full replacement for both gas and electric tank systems, including any venting or clearance corrections needed to bring an older installation up to current Washington State code.

Local Cause

Why Replacement Timing Matters in Older Everett Homes

A tank water heater has a typical service life of 10-12 years. Past that point, internal tank corrosion becomes the limiting factor, not the heating element or thermostat, which means repairs stop being cost-effective even when a specific component can technically still be fixed.

Homes with original galvanized supply lines feeding the heater see accelerated sediment buildup inside the tank, since corrosion particles from the aging pipe accumulate at the tank bottom faster than they would with copper or PEX supply lines. This is one reason a water heater in an older Everett home sometimes needs replacement earlier than the standard 10-12 year window.

Signs

Signs It's Time to Replace, Not Repair

  • The tank is 10 or more years old and now needs its first or second repair.
  • Visible rust-colored water or sediment in the hot water supply.
  • Any sign of water pooling or moisture at the base of the tank.
  • Rising energy bills tied to declining heating efficiency.
  • A household that's outgrown the tank's recovery rate, with hot water regularly running out.
Method

Sizing and Code Check Before Installation

We size the replacement tank to the household's actual hot water demand, not just a like-for-like swap of the existing unit's capacity, since many older installations were undersized even when new.

For gas units, we verify the venting configuration and closet clearance against current code before installation, correcting anything that doesn't meet standard, particularly in homes where the original closet predates modern venting requirements.

Options

Standard Tank, High-Efficiency Tank, or Tankless

A standard tank replacement is the most straightforward option and works well for most households. A high-efficiency tank costs more upfront but reduces energy costs over its service life, particularly relevant given Snohomish County PUD's electricity rates.

For households wanting on-demand hot water and space savings, we'll walk through whether a tankless system fits your home's existing gas or electrical service, since not every home can support a tankless conversion without additional electrical or gas line work.

Pricing

What Affects Replacement Cost in Everett

Cost Factors

  • Tank type: standard, high-efficiency, and tankless units carry different equipment costs.
  • Venting corrections: bringing an older closet up to current code adds labor.
  • Capacity: a larger household needs a higher-capacity or higher-recovery-rate unit.
  • Removal and disposal: removing and properly disposing of the old tank is part of most installations.
  • Permits: Everett requires permitting for water heater replacement.

Standard water heater replacement due to age is a maintenance expense, not typically an insurance claim. Sudden tank failure causing water damage may be covered separately under your homeowner's policy.

Before You Call

What to Know Before Scheduling Replacement

  • Know your tank's age, usually on a manufacturer sticker on the unit, to gauge urgency.
  • Note your household size and typical hot water usage to help size the replacement correctly.
  • Check whether your existing closet has any visible venting or clearance issues.
  • Decide whether you want to explore tankless before committing to another tank replacement.
  • Budget for permitting, which is required for water heater replacement in Everett.
Questions

Water Heater Replacement Questions

How long does a full replacement take?

Most standard tank replacements are completed in a few hours. Jobs requiring venting corrections or electrical work for a tankless conversion take longer.

Is it worth paying more for a high-efficiency tank?

For most households, yes, over the tank's service life, though the upfront cost is higher. We can walk through the real payback timeline for your household's usage.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater?

Yes, Everett requires a permit for water heater replacement, which we handle as part of the installation.

Can you also just repair my current unit instead?

In many cases, yes, if the tank is under 10 years old. See our water heater repair page for repair-specific information.

Do you serve areas outside Everett?

Yes, including Mill Creek, Snohomish, Lake Stevens, Mukilteo, and Edmonds.
