How to Find a Hidden Water Leak Before It Costs You Thousands

The water meter test takes five minutes and tells you more than guessing based on your water bill alone.

By the Everett Pro Plumbing team · Updated 2026-09-19

A hidden leak can run for weeks or months before becoming visible, quietly adding to your water bill and, in the worst cases, causing structural or mold damage long before anyone notices standing water. Catching it early starts with one simple test almost anyone can do at home.

The Water Meter Test

Turn off every water-using fixture and appliance in your home, faucets, toilets, dishwasher, washing machine, ice maker, everything. Then locate your water meter, usually near the street or in a basement utility area, and check whether the dial or digital readout is still moving. If it's moving with everything off, water is escaping somewhere in your system, whether you can see it or not.

Where Hidden Leaks Typically Hide

Toilets are the single most common source of a hidden leak that doesn't show up as visible water, since a worn flapper can leak silently into the bowl. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and wait 15 minutes without flushing; if color appears in the bowl, the toilet is leaking.

Beyond toilets, hidden leaks commonly occur in a wall cavity along a supply line, under a concrete slab, or along the buried water service line between the meter and the house. These require different detection methods than a simple visual check, and any repair follows Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) standards for the pipe material and location involved.

What to Do Once You've Confirmed a Leak

Once the meter test confirms an active leak with no obvious source, the next step is professional acoustic detection, which pinpoints the exact location without needing to open walls or cut concrete on a guess. See our leak detection page for how this process works, and our slab leak detection & repair page specifically if you're seeing a warm spot on a concrete floor, a classic sign of a hot water line leaking underneath.

Why Speed Matters Here

A hidden leak doesn't just waste water. Depending on location, it can promote mold growth in a wall cavity, undermine soil beneath a foundation, or, in an older home with galvanized supply lines, indicate broader system corrosion that's likely to produce more leaks elsewhere. The cost of finding and fixing a leak early is almost always lower than the combined cost of the wasted water and the eventual, larger repair once it becomes visible.

The Bottom Line

The meter test costs nothing and takes five minutes. If it confirms a leak, professional acoustic detection finds the exact location without unnecessary demolition, and the earlier you act, the less the eventual repair and water waste ends up costing.

Reading Your Water Bill for Clues

Beyond the meter test, comparing several months of water bills can reveal a leak's approximate start date and severity. A gradual, steady increase suggests a slowly worsening leak, like a corroding pipe. A sudden jump between two billing periods with no lifestyle change suggests a specific event, like a fitting failure or a new crack developing. This timeline is useful context to share when you do call for professional detection.

Common False Alarms Worth Ruling Out First

Before assuming the worst, rule out a few simple explanations: an irrigation system left running or with a stuck valve, a pool or hot tub actively filling, or a recently changed household routine like extended guests. These aren't hidden leaks in the plumbing sense, but they produce the same symptom, a running meter with no obvious cause inside the house, and they're worth checking first since the fix is much simpler.

Why a Faster Response Saves More Than Money

A hidden leak that continues for months doesn't just add to a water bill. In a wall cavity, sustained moisture creates conditions for mold growth well before any visible staining appears on the surface. Under a slab, prolonged water intrusion can affect soil stability beneath the foundation over time. Neither of these secondary effects show up in the water bill number, which is exactly why catching the leak at the meter-test stage, rather than waiting for a visible symptom, genuinely matters beyond the dollar cost.

What Detection Equipment Actually Finds

Acoustic leak detection works by listening for the distinct sound pressurized water makes escaping through a small opening, a sound that travels clearly through pipe, wall material, and even concrete. This lets a technician narrow a leak's location to a small area, often within a few feet, without needing to guess where to start cutting. For a suspected slab leak specifically, this precision is what keeps a repair to a small, targeted penetration instead of an exploratory search across an entire floor. The alternative, cutting exploratory sections of concrete based on guesswork, is both more expensive and more disruptive than the detection step itself, which is a strong argument for calling before reaching for a hammer or a saw on a guess. The cost of the detection visit is almost always smaller than the cost of an unnecessary hole in the wrong spot.

Quick Answers

How much does a hidden leak typically cost in wasted water? It varies widely by leak size, but even a small, continuous leak can waste hundreds of gallons a month.

Can a plumber find a leak without cutting into anything? In most cases, yes, using acoustic or thermal detection before any cutting starts.

Is it normal for a meter to move slightly even with everything off? A very slow, intermittent movement can sometimes be a toilet fill valve cycling briefly; a steady, continuous movement is not normal and warrants a closer look, ideally with acoustic detection rather than repeated guessing.
