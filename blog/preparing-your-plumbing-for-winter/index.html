Preparing Your Plumbing for Winter in Snohomish County

A few checks in October save a lot of trouble in January. Here's the real, non-generic version of this checklist.

By the Everett Pro Plumbing team · Updated 2026-09-19

Snohomish County's marine climate means winter here looks different from colder inland regions, less about hard freezes and more about sustained rain, rising groundwater, and the occasional cold snap that catches an unprepared system off guard. A short seasonal checklist meaningfully reduces the odds of an emergency call during the worst weeks of the year.

Test Your Sump Pump Now, Not During the First Storm

Pour a bucket of water into the sump basin and confirm the pump activates, moves water through the discharge line, and shuts off correctly. A pump that's been sitting idle since last spring can fail silently, and the first real test shouldn't be during an actual storm. See our sump pump installation & repair page if the test reveals a problem.

Check Downspouts and Grading

Confirm downspouts discharge at least 5-6 feet from the foundation, and check that yard grading slopes away from the house, especially if any landscaping work happened over the summer. This is one of the most overlooked and easiest-to-fix contributors to winter basement flooding.

Insulate Exposed Pipes in Unheated Spaces

While Everett rarely sees hard, sustained freezes, occasional cold snaps do happen, and a pipe running through an uninsulated crawlspace or exterior wall is at real risk during those specific cold periods. Basic foam pipe insulation, available at any hardware store, is an inexpensive way to reduce that risk on exposed sections, and it meets standard code guidelines for freeze protection in unconditioned spaces. Foam sleeves cost only a few dollars per section and take about ten minutes to install on an average crawlspace run.

Know Where Your Main Shutoff Valve Is

This matters every season, but it matters most in winter when a burst pipe is more likely. If you don't already know where your main water shutoff is, find it now, before an emergency, not during one. See our burst pipe repair page for the full first-response sequence if a pipe does fail.

Consider a Basement Waterproofing Assessment If You Have a History

If your basement or crawlspace has flooded in a previous winter, scheduling a real drainage assessment before this season starts is far more useful than repeating the same emergency cleanup again. See our basement waterproofing page for what that assessment actually covers.

The Bottom Line

None of these steps take more than an hour combined, and together they address the specific failure points, sump pumps, drainage, exposed pipe, and unknown shutoff locations, that actually cause most winter plumbing emergencies in this area.

Check Your Water Heater Before the Heavy-Use Season

Winter puts extra demand on a water heater as households use more hot water for longer showers and washing. If your unit has been showing early warning signs, rumbling sounds, reduced hot water volume, or it's simply never been flushed, addressing it before winter demand peaks avoids losing hot water during the coldest, least convenient stretch of the year. See our water heater repair page if you're noticing any of these signs already.

Test Your Backflow Device If You Have One

If your property has an irrigation system with a backflow prevention device, confirm it's been properly winterized if you're shutting the system down for the season, and that annual testing is current heading into the wetter months. See our backflow testing & prevention page for what this involves, and note that a device winterized incorrectly can crack over a hard freeze even if the rest of your plumbing is fine. A cracked backflow device over winter is a common, avoidable spring surprise that a five-minute check in the fall prevents entirely.

Building This Into an Annual Habit

The homeowners who avoid winter plumbing emergencies most consistently aren't the ones with the newest systems, they're the ones who run through the same short checklist every year around the same time, regardless of whether last winter was uneventful. Tying it to another seasonal task you already do, changing furnace filters or testing smoke detectors, makes it easier to remember consistently rather than relying on remembering it cold each fall.

What to Do If You're Already Behind

If the rainy season has already started and you haven't done any of this yet, it's still worth working through the list now rather than waiting until next year. A late sump pump test still catches a failing unit before the worst storm of the season, and late grading fixes still reduce risk for whatever rain is still coming. Being a month behind schedule is better than not checking at all. The goal is reducing risk for whatever portion of the season remains, not achieving a perfect record.

Quick Answers

Do I need to winterize outdoor spigots here? Yes, disconnect hoses and consider an insulated cover, especially for spigots on north-facing exterior walls.

Should I have my furnace and water heater serviced together? It's convenient to schedule both at once, since a technician visiting for one can often flag issues with the other.

Does homeowner's insurance require any of this? Not typically as a policy condition, though following it reduces the odds of filing a claim in the first place.

How cold does it actually get in Everett? Rarely below freezing for extended periods, but brief cold snaps do happen most winters.

Should I leave a faucet dripping during a cold snap? For exposed or previously problematic pipes, yes, a slow drip reduces freeze risk during the coldest nights, since moving water resists freezing better than standing water in the same pipe. This is a short-term measure for a specific cold night, not a substitute for insulating exposed pipe properly.
