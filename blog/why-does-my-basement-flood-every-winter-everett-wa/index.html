Why Your Everett Basement Floods Every Winter (and the Fix)

Basement flooding that repeats every winter isn't bad luck. It's a specific, diagnosable cause, and Snohomish County's climate makes a few causes far more common than others.

By the Everett Pro Plumbing team · Updated 2026-10-12

If your basement or crawlspace floods every year during the October-through-April rainy season, and dries out fine the rest of the year, that pattern itself tells you something important: this isn't a one-time plumbing failure. It's a structural drainage issue that reliably shows up whenever the water table rises, and it's going to keep happening every winter until the actual cause is addressed.

Three common causes account for most recurring winter basement floods, and they're worth understanding separately, since the fix for each is different.

Cause One: Failed Foundation Drain Tile

Many older homes have a perimeter drain tile system installed at the footing level, designed to carry groundwater away from the foundation before it can push through the walls. Over 50-plus years, that original tile silts up or collapses, and once it stops working, hydrostatic pressure from rising winter groundwater has nowhere to go except through the foundation itself.

Cause Two: An Undersized or Failing Sump Pump

A sump pump that was adequate for a typical winter can still be overwhelmed during an unusually wet stretch. A pump that's simply aging out, past its typical 7-10 year service life, will often fail at the exact moment it's needed most: during the heaviest storm of the season. This is a common issue even in newer homes near a high water table, like the areas around Lake Stevens' shoreline, where the site's groundwater conditions matter more than the age of the house itself.

Cause Three: Grading and Downspout Problems

A surprising share of basement flooding traces back to something outside the foundation entirely: downspouts discharging too close to the house, or yard grading that slopes toward the foundation instead of away from it. This is often the easiest and cheapest cause to fix, and it's worth ruling out before assuming you need a major interior drainage project.

How to Tell Which One Applies to You

Timing is the first clue. If flooding happens during active rain, grading and downspouts are the likely culprit. If it happens hours or days after rain stops, that points toward groundwater pressure and failed drain tile. If your sump pump runs constantly but the basement still floods, the pump itself may be undersized for the actual water volume.

A proper diagnosis checks all three rather than guessing based on one visit's symptoms. See our basement waterproofing page for how we assess and fix foundation drainage, and our sump pump installation & repair page if the pump itself is the likely weak link.

Why This Matters More in Snohomish County

Snohomish County's long rainy season keeps the water table high for months, which is why recurring basement flooding is a real pattern here. Treating the symptom every winter costs more over time than fixing the actual drainage cause once.

Why the Timing Pattern Is Worth Tracking

If your basement has flooded more than once, start writing down when it happens relative to the rain, not just that it happened. Flooding that starts within an hour of heavy rain beginning points toward surface water and grading. Flooding that shows up a day or two after a multi-day storm points toward groundwater pressure building up gradually and finally overwhelming the foundation drainage. This distinction alone can save you from paying for the wrong fix.

It's also worth noting whether the water appears to be coming from a specific wall or corner versus rising evenly across the floor. A single problem area often points to a localized crack or a failed section of drain tile, while even flooding across the whole floor suggests a broader groundwater pressure issue affecting the entire foundation.

The Cost of Waiting

A basement that floods every winter and gets cleaned up every spring is easy to treat as a seasonal inconvenience rather than a problem worth fixing permanently. But repeated flooding causes cumulative damage: mold growth in wall cavities, deterioration of stored belongings, and in severe cases, structural stress on the foundation itself from repeated hydrostatic pressure cycles. The cost of addressing the actual cause once is almost always lower than the combined cost of repeated cleanup, lost belongings, and eventual larger repairs.

What a Real Assessment Looks Like

A proper diagnosis doesn't stop at the first visible sign of water. It includes checking exterior grading and downspout placement, testing existing sump pump capacity against the basin's actual water volume, and, where warranted, assessing the condition of any original perimeter drain tile. Skipping any one of these steps risks fixing the wrong cause and having the same flood happen again next winter.

What to Do Before the Next Storm

A few checks now, before the rainy season peaks, can prevent a repeat of last year's flood or at least tell you what to expect:

  • Confirm downspouts discharge at least 5-6 feet from the foundation, not directly against it.
  • Test your sump pump manually by pouring water into the basin and watching it cycle.
  • Check that yard grading slopes away from the foundation, especially after any landscaping work.
  • Note where past flooding entered, a specific wall, a floor drain, or evenly across the floor.
  • If you smell a musty odor without visible water, treat it as an early sign, not a false alarm.

Basement Flooding in Everett: County and NOAA Facts

NOAA's 1991-2020 normals for the Everett Snohomish County Airport station put about 26 of the year's 33 inches of rain between October and April. Snohomish County's Before a Flood tips say homeowners insurance does not cover flood loss, that check valves can keep floodwater out of drains, and that Planning and Development Services can tell you whether a property is flood-prone.

Snohomish County Surface Water Management also takes requests for a drainage investigation. For basements in Snohomish or Lake Stevens, see our basement waterproofing and sump pump pages.

Quick Answers

Is basement flooding covered by insurance?

Sudden events sometimes are. Gradual groundwater seepage typically is not. Check your policy's specific seepage language.

Can I fix this myself?

Grading and downspout adjustments are DIY-friendly. Interior drain tile and foundation work generally require a professional.

How fast does a fix typically pay for itself?

Most homeowners recover the cost within a few winters through avoided cleanup, repairs, and replaced belongings.

Does homeowners insurance cover basement flooding in Snohomish County?

Snohomish County's Before a Flood guidance says homeowners insurance does not cover flood loss. Ask your insurance agent exactly what your policy covers, and ask Planning and Development Services whether your property is flood-prone.

The Bottom Line

Recurring winter flooding has a real, findable cause, whether it's failed drain tile, an undersized pump, or simple grading. Tracking the timing and location of each flood gives a professional the clues needed to diagnose it correctly on the first visit, rather than guessing and hoping. Left unaddressed, the same drainage failure tends to worsen slightly each year as the underlying tile or grading issue continues to deteriorate.
