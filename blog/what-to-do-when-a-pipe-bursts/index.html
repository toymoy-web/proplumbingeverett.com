What to Do in the First 10 Minutes After a Pipe Bursts

Speed matters, but doing things in the wrong order can cost you more than the delay itself.

By the Everett Pro Plumbing team · Updated 2026-09-19

A burst pipe is one of the few plumbing emergencies where the first few minutes genuinely affect how much damage you end up with. The good news is the correct sequence of steps is simple and doesn't require any special knowledge, just doing them in the right order under pressure.

Step 1: Shut Off the Main Water Valve

Before anything else, before calling anyone, find and shut off your home's main water shutoff valve. This is the single most important action you can take, since it stops the source of the damage rather than just reacting to it. The main shutoff is typically located where the water line enters the house, often in a basement, crawlspace, or utility closet, sometimes near the water heater.

If you don't already know where yours is, this is worth finding right now, before an emergency, not during one. A few minutes spent searching for the valve while water is actively flowing is exactly the delay that turns a manageable leak into serious water damage.

Step 2: Address Electrical Safety

If water is pooling near outlets, switches, or any electrical fixture, shut off power to that area at the breaker panel before doing anything else in that space. Water and electricity are a genuine safety hazard, and this takes priority over cleanup.

Step 3: Call for Emergency Repair

Once the water is off and any electrical hazard addressed, call for emergency repair. A burst pipe from aging galvanized steel, common in Everett's pre-1970s homes, is rarely an isolated event. The section that failed has usually been narrowing and weakening for years alongside the rest of the system, so a proper repair should include checking nearby pipe condition, not just patching the visible break.

See our burst pipe repair page for what to expect from the diagnosis and repair process itself, including how we decide between a spot repair and a broader section replacement.

Step 4: Document for Insurance

Once the immediate danger is handled, photograph the damage before starting any cleanup. Sudden, accidental water damage from a burst pipe is typically covered under standard homeowner's insurance, but you'll want clear documentation of the extent of damage before anything gets moved or dried.

Step 5: Start Moving Water and Valuables

If it's safe to do so, move furniture, electronics, and valuables away from standing water while you wait for repair. Every extra minute water sits against flooring, drywall, or furniture increases the secondary damage beyond what the burst itself caused.

This sequence applies anywhere in our service area, but it's especially relevant in Edmonds' historic Bowl neighborhood, where a large share of homes still run the same pre-1950s galvanized supply lines that make a sudden burst more likely in the first place. See our leak detection page if you're dealing with a slower, less obvious leak rather than a full burst.

What Not to Do

Don't wait to see if a small leak gets worse before shutting off the water. A pipe that's already failed at one point rarely seals itself, and the flow rate from even a modest crack adds up to hundreds of gallons over just a few hours. Don't attempt to patch a burst pipe yourself with tape or a clamp as a permanent fix, since these are, at best, a temporary measure until a proper repair, not a substitute for one.

Also avoid running any appliance connected to the affected line, dishwashers, washing machines, ice makers, until the repair is complete, since doing so can reintroduce pressure to a section of pipe that's already compromised.

After the Repair: What to Watch For

Once the immediate repair is complete, keep an eye on the area for the next few weeks. Persistent dampness, a musty smell, or any new staining can indicate the drying process wasn't fully complete, or that a secondary issue, like mold starting in a wall cavity, needs separate attention. A burst pipe from aging galvanized supply lines is also a signal worth taking seriously for the rest of your home's plumbing, not just the one section that failed.

When One Burst Pipe Means More Are Coming

A single burst in an otherwise sound copper or PEX system is often just bad luck, a manufacturing defect or physical damage at one spot. A burst in a galvanized system is different: the same corrosion process that weakened the section that failed has been working on the rest of the system for the same number of decades. If your home is on original galvanized supply lines, treat one burst as a signal to have the whole system assessed, not just the one repaired section.

Quick Answers

Can I use tape as a permanent fix? No, tape or a repair clamp is a temporary measure only, never a substitute for a proper repair.

Is a burst pipe always an emergency? Yes, treat it as one. Even a small, slow burst adds up to significant water volume over just a few hours.

What if I can't find my main shutoff? Locate it now, before an emergency, typically near where the water line enters the house.

The Bottom Line

Speed matters after a burst pipe, but doing things in the right order matters more. Shut off the water first, address any electrical hazard second, then call. Everything else, documentation, moving valuables, can happen once the immediate danger is under control. Keep this sequence somewhere accessible, a phone note or a card near the water shutoff, so it's there when you need it most. The five steps above take less time to follow than it takes to read them once, which is the point.
